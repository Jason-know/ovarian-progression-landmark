TITLE|Progression Status and Timing for Updating Mortality Risk in Advanced Serous Ovarian Cancer
SUBTITLE|A landmark study with three independent external validations
H1|Abstract
P|**Objective.** To determine whether the timing of first documented progression adds prognostic information beyond progression status at a fixed follow-up visit in advanced serous ovarian cancer.
P|**Methods.** In adults with stage III–IV serous tubo-ovarian or primary peritoneal carcinoma alive and observed at month 12, we predicted all-cause death over the next 12 months. We fitted three nested censoring-weighted logistic models in TCGA. The models included age, FIGO stage and postoperative residual disease, then added progression status and finally time since first documented progression. We validated the locked models in the Australian Ovarian Cancer Study (AOCS), University Medical Center Hamburg-Eppendorf (UKE), and a public ICON7 trial subset.
P|**Results.** The analysis included 850 patients and 142 deaths by the primary horizon. Across AOCS, UKE and ICON7, respectively, adding progression status increased time-dependent area under the curve (AUC) by 0.219 (95% CI, 0.107 to 0.344), 0.235 (0.096 to 0.389), and 0.180 (0.069 to 0.290). Adding progression timing changed AUC by −0.005 (−0.015 to 0.005), −0.005 (−0.015 to 0.003), and 0.001 (−0.004 to 0.005). The status model had external AUCs of 0.855, 0.759, and 0.852. Ratios of observed to expected deaths ranged from 1.26 to 2.12.
P|**Conclusions.** Adding progression status at month 12 consistently improved discrimination. Recording the exact interval since progression yielded little additional discrimination at this landmark. Systematic underprediction in external cohorts limits use of the current equation for individual risk estimates.
P|**Keywords.** Serous ovarian carcinoma, landmark analysis, disease progression, overall survival, external validation, calibration.
H1|Introduction
P|Age, FIGO stage and residual disease after cytoreductive surgery help characterize prognosis at diagnosis. Residual tumor burden shows a graded relationship with survival [1]. By a later follow-up visit, the patient's observed disease course may provide information that was unavailable after surgery. A probability estimate based only on baseline predictors can therefore miss distinctions that are relevant to survivorship counseling and study stratification.
P|Existing ovarian cancer studies have estimated conditional survival using elapsed survival or progression-free time [2,3]. Investigators have also evaluated serial CA-125 in dynamic prediction models [4,5], explored recurrent disease characteristics for prediction from the time of relapse [6,7], and reported a single-center longitudinal AI model that included early recurrence [8]. These approaches motivate a direct comparison within the same landmark population. Once the clinician knows whether the patient has progressed, it remains useful to ask whether the date of that progression improves subsequent mortality prediction enough to travel across settings.
P|We compared models with identical risk sets and progressively richer disease-course information. We used the same locked TCGA equations across independent observational and trial-derived cohorts. We evaluated ranking and absolute risk separately, because improved discrimination can coexist with miscalibration when a model is transported.
H1|Methods
H2|Study design and data sources
P|We performed a retrospective analysis of de-identified patient-level data from previously published studies. TCGA-OV was the development cohort. Diagnosis, survival and progression-free interval (PFI) came from the TCGA Pan-Cancer Clinical Data Resource (CDR). Postoperative residual diameter came from the January 2016 Broad GDAC Firehose merged clinical file joined by TCGA patient barcode [9,10]. External validation used AOCS (GSE9891), the public UKE Supplementary Table S1b, and the publicly deposited ICON7 translational subset (GSE140082) [11–15]. We used GSE32062 (Japanese data set A) for an age-free analysis because the series matrix lacked individual ages [16]. No gene expression measurements entered any model. Supplementary Table S1 specifies each source mapping.
P|Eligible patients had stage III or IV invasive serous epithelial disease, including ovarian, fallopian tube and primary peritoneal primaries where represented in the original publications. We used source histology labels without imposing a common grade restriction. We selected recorded platinum treatment without neoadjuvant chemotherapy in AOCS, platinum with or without bevacizumab in UKE, and the posted standard or bevacizumab arm in ICON7. Platinum or maintenance exposure was not a model covariate. TCGA standardized endpoints did not provide harmonized treatment exposure for these filters. In UKE we excluded samples annotated as recurrent tumor and collapsed concordant sample records to one record per patient. We excluded TCGA rows flagged as redacted.
H2|Landmark predictors and outcome
P|We set the primary landmark at 12 months after the origin for each source. TCGA and AOCS used diagnosis-based follow-up. UKE reported follow-up from initial diagnosis. ICON7 endpoints used trial randomization. The Japanese secondary analysis used the published PFS and OS clocks. We used positive OS follow-up beyond the landmark and either an event by the landmark or documented progression-free observation through it. This restriction avoids using a later progression date to classify an earlier clinical state. Follow-up ended at death from any cause or at last recorded contact. Death within 12 months after the landmark was the primary outcome. We selected that horizon after reviewing follow-up coverage, before fitting prognostic models.
P|Predictors were age at diagnosis in decades centered at 60, stage IV versus III, and suboptimal cytoreduction. TCGA residual diameter was dichotomized at 10 mm. AOCS and UKE used the recorded residual size categories, and ICON7 and Japan used source-defined optimal or suboptimal cytoreduction. No macroscopic residual and small-volume residual were combined. Dimensions recorded as unknown could not be assigned to the primary groups. We analyzed complete records and repeated model fitting without the residual predictor to assess the effect of this eligibility decision.
P|We defined early progression as a source-recorded progression or relapse event at or before month 12. Time since first progression was calculated as the landmark minus the first recorded event time. We preserved months supplied by the sources and converted days to months using 365.25 divided by 12. Some PFS or PFI definitions also count death. A participant who had died before or at the landmark could not enter the risk set. We repeated model fitting excluding TCGA patients with early PFI events lacking an explicit recurrence or progression label. These time intervals are anchored to each source's origin and do not encode the time from the last platinum dose.
H2|Association and model assessment
P|We displayed Kaplan–Meier survival after month 12 by no early progression, progression less than six months before the landmark, or progression at least six months before the landmark. We fitted Cox models separately in each cohort, administratively truncating follow-up 24 months after the landmark, and adjusted for age, stage and residual disease. We used rank-transformed Schoenfeld tests to examine proportional hazards. We interpreted sparse subgroup models and interaction tests descriptively. Exploratory restricted cubic splines used knots at 0, 6 and 12 months since progression and displayed the central 90% of observed timing. The reference prediction profile was age 60, stage III and optimal cytoreduction.
P|At the same landmark we fitted three nested logistic models. Baseline included age, stage and residual disease. Status added early progression. Timing added (months since progression − 6) divided by 6 among those with progression, with zero for the remaining participants. This separates whether progression occurred from its timing among those who progressed. The main logistic models used weak L2 regularization (inverse strength C = 100). We modeled fixed-horizon outcomes with inverse probability of censoring weighting (IPCW). We estimated the marginal censoring survival G by Kaplan–Meier, assigning observed deaths by the horizon a weight of 1/G just before death and participants observed beyond the horizon 1/G at the horizon. Participants censored before or at the horizon contributed zero weight. This approach assumes independent right censoring within the evaluated cohort [17–19].
P|We assessed internal performance using stratified five-fold out-of-fold predictions. The complete TCGA fits were then applied to each external cohort without refitting the coefficients, intercept or predictor scaling. We evaluated cumulative/dynamic AUC, IPCW Brier score, observed-to-expected ratio, calibration intercept and slope, and decision curves. The primary contrast was the change in AUC from Status to Timing. We separately evaluated Status versus Baseline. External 95% percentile intervals used 1,000 paired patient bootstrap resamples, re-estimating the censoring distribution in each resample and holding the fitted development equations fixed. In external validation the intervals therefore quantify sampling variation in the evaluation cohort. Decision curves use hypothetical risk flagging thresholds from 5% to 45% [20,21].
P|We compared eight fixed algorithms using the Timing feature set. They comprised weakly penalized logistic regression, LASSO logistic regression, elastic net, a decision tree, random forest, extremely randomized trees, gradient boosting and histogram gradient boosting. We compared LASSO coefficient paths and a forest shadow-feature diagnostic in TCGA. The shadow procedure used 90 independent permutations and identified predictors exceeding the maximum shadow importance in at least 90% of runs. The LASSO diagnostic selected the penalty minimizing five-fold IPCW Brier score. These diagnostics did not determine primary model predictors. We also compared a progression-only benchmark after reviewing the main results.
P|Sensitivity analyses evaluated landmarks at 6 and 18 months, a 24-month prediction horizon, exclusion of events at the recorded landmark, documented TCGA progression events, removal of age or residual disease, and standard-treatment subsets. We used 350 patient bootstrap resamples for exploratory external sensitivity intervals. We followed the applicable reporting items from TRIPOD+AI [22]. The retrospective analysis plan was written after a data feasibility audit and before model fitting.
H1|Results
H2|Participants and landmark prognosis
P|The primary risk set included 347 TCGA, 133 AOCS, 146 UKE and 224 ICON7 patients (Table 1 and Figure S4). Across cohorts, 15.8% to 32.3% had documented progression by month 12. The number of observed deaths by the next 12 months was 50, 30, 29 and 33, respectively. Reverse Kaplan–Meier median follow-up after the landmark ranged from 15.6 months in ICON7 to 70.3 months in TCGA.
P|Landmark survival curves separated by progression status. Estimated 12-month mortality in participants with early progression was 33.5% in TCGA, 59.0% in AOCS, 63.0% in UKE and 49.3% in ICON7. Corresponding estimates for participants without documented progression at month 12 were 7.2%, 5.8%, 12.3% and 6.4% (Figures 1 and 2). The adjusted hazard ratios for early progression were 4.24 (95% CI, 2.87 to 6.28), 9.32 (4.70 to 18.52), 8.26 (4.28 to 15.94), and 7.03 (4.08 to 12.11). Adjusted associations with progression timing varied by cohort. In AOCS the hazard ratio per six additional months since progression was 5.19 (1.66 to 16.28), compared with 0.88 (0.43 to 1.81) in TCGA, 3.07 (0.48 to 19.72) in UKE and 0.74 (0.24 to 2.30) in ICON7. Some ICON7 Schoenfeld tests flagged possible nonproportionality, so its hazard ratios summarize follow-up rather than a constant effect. Subgroup estimates were imprecise, and two AOCS strata yielded unstable fits (Figure 6).
H2|Added predictive value
P|Internal out-of-fold AUCs for Baseline, Status and Timing were 0.616, 0.757 and 0.747. In each external cohort the Status model improved the ranking of 12-month risk relative to Baseline, with AUC increases of 0.180 to 0.235 and confidence intervals above zero. The primary Timing versus Status contrast was near zero in all three external cohorts (Table 2). Brier scores were lower with Status than with Baseline by 0.045 in AOCS, 0.013 in UKE and 0.024 in ICON7. The UKE interval for that Brier difference included zero. Brier score changes after adding Timing were between −0.001 and 0.002 across external cohorts. In the post hoc progression-only comparison, adding baseline features to progression status improved AUC in all three external cohorts. UKE Brier score was 0.143 with progression alone and 0.153 with the combined Status model.
P|Across the eight Timing algorithms, external AUCs ranged from 0.833 to 0.866 in AOCS, 0.615 to 0.771 in UKE and 0.762 to 0.860 in ICON7 (Figure 4 and Supplementary Table S2). The AUC ordering varied across cohorts. LASSO retained all five predictors at its minimum Brier penalty. Age, progression status and progression timing exceeded the shadow-feature criterion (Figure 3). Progression timing is zero-coded for patients without progression and consequently carries information about progression status. Its apparent importance in that screen does not establish incremental benefit over the Status model.
H2|Calibration and sensitivity analyses
P|The Status model underpredicted absolute risk in all external cohorts, with observed-to-expected ratios of 1.39 in AOCS, 2.12 in UKE and 1.26 in ICON7. External calibration slopes were 1.50, 1.25 and 1.36. In UKE the observed and expected 12-month risks were 20.1% and 9.5%. The grouped calibration and threshold-dependent decision curves illustrate the practical consequences of transporting unadjusted probabilities (Figure 5). Model coefficients and a graphical representation of the Status equation are provided for reproducibility (Figure S1).
P|The discrimination gain from adding Status persisted across sensitivity analyses (Figure S3). The reduced-feature Status model achieved an AUC of 0.820 (95% CI, 0.743 to 0.888) in the Japanese cohort, compared with 0.524 for its Baseline model and 0.810 after adding Timing. The reduced model overpredicted risk there, with an observed-to-expected ratio of 0.68. At the 18-month landmark, the Timing contrast was 0.049 (0.007 to 0.095) in AOCS and smaller with confidence intervals spanning zero in UKE and ICON7. At the 24-month prediction horizon, a large fraction of ICON7 participants had been censored (estimated G = 0.207 and 28 observed controls), limiting the precision of that sensitivity analysis.
H1|Discussion
P|In four cohorts of advanced serous ovarian cancer, progression status at month 12 separated risk of subsequent death and consistently improved ranking over age, stage and surgical residual disease. The exact time since progression did not materially improve the locked model at the primary landmark. The AOCS timing association and its later-landmark discrimination gain suggest that timing can matter in a particular setting. These effects did not generalize across the three external populations at the primary prediction time.
P|Existing conditional survival studies show that remaining alive or progression-free changes expected survival [2,3]. Studies starting at recurrence address another question about patients who have all relapsed [6,7]. Our comparison starts at a fixed follow-up time and preserves patients with and without progression in the same risk set. It distinguishes the value of recording that progression has occurred from the value of measuring the exact elapsed interval. Its main contribution is the external comparison of those nested information sets and their calibration at a common prediction horizon. The estimates do not establish equivalence for other landmarks, histologies or treatment eras.
P|The better ranking suggests a role for recorded disease course in prospective evaluation of follow-up counseling or stratification of research cohorts. The systematic difference between observed and expected risk means the uncalibrated equation cannot provide reliable individual probabilities across sites. The TCGA point estimate for stage IV was negative in the fitted Status model. Conditioning on survival to month 12, small numbers within predictor strata and clinical selection may contribute to this direction. It should not be interpreted as a protective stage effect. Standardizing progression ascertainment and collecting performance status, response measures and modern maintenance exposure would help evaluate transportability before any clinical use.
P|This is a prognostic analysis. The decision curves characterize how predictions trade off false and true risk flags across hypothetical thresholds. They do not evaluate the effect of changing surveillance or initiating treatment. The randomized MRC OV05/EORTC 55955 trial found no survival advantage from starting treatment at CA-125 rise alone [23]. Contemporary maintenance strategies and guidelines also change the clinical context in which progression is detected and treated [24,25].
P|The study is limited by historic, selected tissue-profiled samples. Source time origins, progression definitions, assessment schedules and date precision differed. The ICON7 subset represents a translational sample from the parent trial and had short observable post-landmark follow-up. UKE included first-line bevacizumab, but harmonized PARP inhibitor, BRCA/HRD, performance status, post-progression therapy, and treatment-free interval data were unavailable. Histologic grades were not harmonized. Complete-case eligibility may select against patients with unknown residual diameter. The censoring analysis assumes independent censoring and may be affected by follow-up practices. The conditional target excludes early deaths and patients lost before month 12. It does not generalize to prognosis at diagnosis. Finally, each external cohort had fewer than 35 observed deaths at the primary horizon, limiting the precision of calibration and subgroup analyses.
H1|Conclusions
P|Recording progression status at month 12 improved external mortality risk ranking in advanced serous ovarian cancer. The additional timing term provided little incremental discrimination at the primary landmark. Contemporary prospective cohorts and external recalibration are necessary to assess use of individual mortality probabilities.
H1|Data availability and ethics
P|TCGA CDR and the Broad GDAC Firehose ovarian clinical archive, AOCS clinical annotations and GSE9891, the UKE publisher supplement, and GEO accessions GSE140082 and GSE32062 are publicly accessible. Source URLs, checksums, predictor mappings, model coefficients and executable analysis scripts accompany this analysis. This secondary study used de-identified public records and involved no participant contact. Ethics approvals and consent procedures for original data collection are described by the source investigators.
