TITLE|Incremental Value of Progression Timing for Mortality Prediction in Advanced Serous Tubo-Ovarian Carcinoma
SUBTITLE|A landmark prediction study with external validation and a tissue pathology analysis
H1|Abstract
P|**Objective.** To assess the incremental value of the interval since first progression when progression status at a fixed follow-up visit is already known.
P|**Methods.** In patients with stage III–IV serous tubo-ovarian or primary peritoneal carcinoma alive and observed at month 12, we predicted death over the following 12 months. We trained censoring-weighted logistic models in TCGA and locked the equations before validation in AOCS, UKE and the public ICON7 tissue subset. We compared clinical baseline predictors, progression status alone, their combination, and addition of progression timing. Post-review analyses evaluated calibration uncertainty, nonlinear timing, pathology and additional cohorts.
P|**Results.** The primary analysis included 850 patients and 142 deaths by the prediction horizon. Adding progression status to age, stage and residual disease increased external AUC by 0.180–0.235. Compared with progression status alone, the combined model increased AUC by 0.031, 0.054 and 0.075 in AOCS, UKE and ICON7, respectively. The paired interval excluded zero only in ICON7, and none showed clear improvement in Brier score. Adding progression timing changed external AUC by −0.005 to 0.001, with two-stage resampling intervals spanning zero. Observed-to-expected mortality ratios for the combined model were 1.39, 2.12 and 1.26. In 342 TCGA patients with linked pathology, adding stromal cell percentage changed out-of-fold AUC by −0.0008 (95% CI, −0.0048 to 0.0025). APOLLO-2 provided independent pathology-cohort evidence based on five deaths.
P|**Conclusions.** Progression status improved short-term risk ranking over baseline characteristics. Adding a fixed timing term or TCGA stromal percentage yielded little additional discrimination. Absolute mortality risk requires validation and calibration in the intended clinical population.
P|**Keywords.** Serous tubo-ovarian carcinoma, disease progression, landmark analysis, mortality, calibration, digital pathology.
H1|Introduction
P|A postoperative prognosis uses information available at surgery, including stage and residual disease [1]. When two otherwise similar patients return after one year, their recorded disease courses may differ. One may already have progressed and the other may still have no documented progression. A further question arises when both patients have progressed. Does knowing the exact date of progression improve the estimate of their mortality risk for the coming year?
P|Su and colleagues evaluated progression-free status and subsequent survival at 9, 12, 15 and 18 months after initiation of platinum-based chemotherapy in 487 patients with BRCA wild-type advanced epithelial ovarian cancer [2]. Earlier conditional survival work quantified how prognosis changes with elapsed survival or progression-free time [3]. A validated nomogram used time to recurrence among patients already at relapse [4]. Longitudinal work has also used an early recurrence indicator or serial CA-125 to estimate survival [5, 6]. These findings establish the context for testing the incremental predictive value of the exact progression interval beyond a binary status measured in the same risk set.
P|We compared the information added by progression status, baseline characteristics and the interval since documented progression. We evaluated portability of both relative ranking and absolute risk using fixed TCGA equations. We also tested whether a continuous tissue pathology measure added prognostic information and whether the clinical associations could be examined in a separate, pathology-reviewed high-grade serous cohort.
H1|Methods
H2|Participants and study design
P|This retrospective individual-level study included adults with source-recorded stage III–IV serous ovarian, fallopian tube or primary peritoneal carcinoma. TCGA-OV was the development set. AOCS, UKE and the public ICON7 AGO-OVAR11 tissue subset were the three primary external evaluation sets (Table 1). Data sources were the TCGA Pan-Cancer Clinical Data Resource (CDR) and Broad Firehose surgical residual fields, Tothill clinical annotations, UKE Supplementary Table S1b and GSE140082 [7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12]. TCGA redacted records were excluded. AOCS required primary surgery without recorded neoadjuvant therapy and receipt of platinum. The UKE and ICON7 cohorts included patients treated with first-line platinum chemotherapy with or without bevacizumab. UKE recurrent tumor specimens were excluded and duplicate primary specimens collapsed to the patient level with concordant clinical values. No gene expression measurements were used as predictors.
P|The target population comprised patients with source-recorded serous carcinoma. Historical numeric grading and the ICON7 source high-grade annotation were used in separate sensitivity analyses. UKE lacked a harmonized individual grade field. The public GEO ICON7 dataset contained 380 records, whereas 359 were selected for the original molecular subtype analysis. We screened the public clinical records for our own endpoint and covariate requirements. The resulting tissue subset should be distinguished from the full international trial [11, 12].
H2|Time origin and endpoints
P|The landmark was 12 months after the start of each source's survival clock. AOCS and TCGA used diagnosis-based follow-up. UKE reported follow-up from initial diagnosis. ICON7 used randomization. This created comparable follow-up intervals with cohort-specific clinical origins (Table 1). Eligible patients were known to be alive beyond the landmark and had a qualifying event by the landmark or progression-free observation through it. The outcome was all-cause death during the 12 months after the landmark.
P|The binary progression indicator used the first event recorded in the available clinical series at or before the landmark. Where a source's composite endpoint includes death, death by the landmark precludes cohort entry. TCGA CDR PFI can also count a new tumor event or death with tumor. We evaluated exclusion of the TCGA patients with a pre-landmark PFI event lacking an explicit recurrence or progression label. ICON7 protocol assessments included months 9 and 12 and used clinical or radiographic progression criteria. The recorded first progression interval therefore reflects each source's ascertainment schedule. It is distinct from time since completion of chemotherapy or time from the last platinum dose [7, 12].
H2|Prediction models and validation
P|We used the same individuals and prediction horizon to compare four information sets. Baseline included age per 10 years centered at 60, stage IV versus III, and postoperative residual disease greater than 1 cm or source-defined suboptimal cytoreduction. Progression-only included the binary indicator. Status combined the baseline variables and progression indicator. Timing added months since first recorded progression, centered at six months and divided by six among patients with progression and set to zero in the remainder. The original three fitted TCGA equations remained fixed for external evaluation. The progression-only model was added as a post-review benchmark and fitted in the same development set.
P|We used weakly penalized logistic regression (L2, inverse strength C = 100) with inverse probability of censoring weights and five-fold stratified out-of-fold predictions within TCGA. For each cohort we estimated the marginal censoring distribution by Kaplan–Meier, weighting observed deaths by the inverse censoring survival just before death and participants observed beyond the prediction horizon by the inverse censoring survival at the horizon. Patients censored before or at the horizon contributed zero weight. We evaluated cumulative/dynamic AUC, Brier score, observed-to-expected (O/E) mortality, and calibration slope and intercept [13, 14, 15, 16, 17]. We estimated unadjusted landmark mortality using Kaplan–Meier and summarized adjusted associations using Cox models through 24 months after the landmark. Standardized source and outcome mappings and original coefficients accompany the analysis.
P|The original primary contrast was Timing versus Status. We retained its paired evaluation bootstrap and added a post-review two-stage bootstrap. Each of 600 replicates resampled TCGA, refitted the nested equations, and separately resampled each external cohort. We also assessed an exploratory natural spline timing term with knots at 0, 6 and 12 months since progression in the same resampling design. Calibration confidence intervals used the fixed Status predictions with the external patients resampled. Performance comparisons were repeated among patients who had already progressed and with censoring weights estimated separately by progression group. We tested adjacent landmarks at months 11 and 13 to assess sensitivity to the boundary. In each adjacent analysis the models were refitted in the corresponding TCGA risk set. Other original analyses included landmarks at 6 and 18 months, a 24-month prediction horizon, removal of age or surgical residual information, documented TCGA events, strict landmark boundaries, and standard-treatment subsets. Decision curves describe hypothetical flagging thresholds. The eight-algorithm comparison and screening diagnostics are secondary (Supplementary Figures S2–S5) [18].
H2|Tissue pathology analysis
P|We linked primary tumor TCGA specimen quality-control slide annotations to development patients by TCGA barcode [19, 20]. For each patient we used the median annotated percentage of stromal cells across slides with valid observations. The post-review comparison used stromal percentage per 10 percentage points as a single additional variable, identical complete cases and the same folds for both predictions. We used paired resampling of fixed out-of-fold predictions to quantify the AUC change. A 24-month Cox model included the clinical Status predictors and stromal percentage. This comparison assesses incremental information from the available slide-level quality-control scores.
P|To document the source measurements, we downloaded two public TCGA H&E tissue slide files whose individual quality-control labels recorded 5% and 35% stromal cells. Selection used the available slide annotation and file availability without using subsequent individual outcomes. Figure 3 shows illustrative tissue fields cropped from the original slides with scale bars derived from scanner metadata. No stain normalization or segmentation was performed. The 5% and 35% values apply to the source slide annotations. They were not calculated from the illustrated fields.
P|We also applied the locked clinical equations to APOLLO-2, which provided expert pathology estimates of tissue composition and diagnosis-based PFS and OS for 70 high-grade serous cases. The workbook distinguished primary and metastatic sampling sites and recorded visible residual disease as R0 or greater than 1 cm. We evaluated the cohort as an exploratory external source and repeated the comparison in the primary-site specimen subset. We did not apply the TCGA pathology coefficient to APOLLO-2 because scoring and sampling differ and the number of deaths was small [21].
H2|Additional cohort analyses
P|We evaluated GSE30161 (UVA) using the full TCGA equations and available published clinical annotations. We excluded one late-stage serous record with a progression interval more than two days longer than its OS interval. In GSE26193 (Curie), age and postoperative residual size were unavailable. In GSE49997 (OVCAD), the source distinguishes any macroscopic residual disease from no residual, so we omitted this variable. The corresponding stage-only and age–stage baseline models were fitted in TCGA with the same follow-up information sets. OVCAD involved Hamburg and may share recruitment with UKE, so it was treated as a separate sensitivity analysis with possible overlap. GSE32062 (Japan) supported an age-free model [22, 23, 24, 25, 26]. We did not pool these source-specific assessments.
H1|Results
H2|Risk separation at the landmark
P|The primary analysis included 347 TCGA, 133 AOCS, 146 UKE and 224 ICON7 patients. A total of 142 observed deaths occurred within one year of the landmark. Progression was recorded by month 12 in 15.8%–32.3% across cohorts. Estimated one-year mortality with progression was 33.5%, 59.0%, 63.0% and 49.3%, respectively, compared with 7.2%, 5.8%, 12.3% and 6.4% without documented progression (Figure 1). The baseline-adjusted progression hazard ratios over 24 months following the landmark were 4.24, 9.32, 8.26 and 7.03. In TCGA the month-12 comparison included 98 early events with a documented recurrence or progression label and five early PFI events without such a label. Excluding the latter from fitting yielded similar discrimination results.
H2|What additional information changed performance
P|Adding progression status to the baseline model increased external AUC by 0.219 in AOCS, 0.235 in UKE and 0.180 in ICON7. Status model AUCs were 0.855, 0.759 and 0.852. When progression status alone was the reference, baseline covariates increased AUC by 0.031, 0.054 and 0.075, respectively. The 95% paired interval exceeded zero only in ICON7. No external Brier improvement was clear relative to progression alone. In UKE the Brier score increased from 0.143 with progression alone to 0.153 with the combined model (Table 2 and Figure 2).
P|Adding the exact interval since first progression to Status changed AUC by −0.005, −0.005 and 0.001. Resampling both development and evaluation patients widened the 95% intervals to −0.056 to 0.021, −0.034 to 0.025 and −0.023 to 0.008. The nonlinear timing sensitivity also had intervals spanning zero in all three main external cohorts. Among patients already progressed, AUC moved from 0.548 to 0.520 (AOCS, n = 43), 0.398 to 0.380 (UKE, n = 23) and 0.636 to 0.648 (ICON7, n = 52). The paired intervals spanned zero in these smaller subsets. Status model AUCs computed using censoring weights stratified by progression status were 0.860, 0.761 and 0.853, close to the main estimates.
P|Status gains over Baseline persisted at the month 11 and 13 landmarks. In the source-defined high-grade ICON7 subset (n = 169), adding status increased AUC by 0.219. In historical grade 3 AOCS records (n = 85), the increment was 0.244. The time-term increment was close to zero in both groups. At month 18 AOCS showed an exploratory positive timing increment of 0.049, while UKE and ICON7 estimates were smaller with intervals spanning zero. Higher censoring at the 24-month prediction horizon in public ICON7 limited longer-term evaluation.
H2|Absolute risk and extended evaluation
P|The locked Status model underpredicted in the three primary validation samples by point estimate. O/E ratios were 1.39 (95% CI, 1.02–1.75) in AOCS, 2.12 (1.48–2.73) in UKE and 1.26 (0.91–1.65) in ICON7. In UKE one-year mortality was 20.1% by censoring-adjusted estimation and 9.5% by mean predicted probability. Calibration intercepts and slopes are reported in Supplementary Table S3. An inverse stage IV coefficient appeared in the locked short-horizon TCGA equation. Of the unredacted TCGA stage III–IV serous records before the landmark restrictions, 17 of 78 stage IV and 30 of 415 stage III patients died before or at the landmark. This source pattern supports reporting the conditional target and avoids interpreting the fitted stage coefficient as a protective effect.
P|In the additional UVA dataset (39 patients, nine deaths), locked model AUC was 0.402 for Baseline, 0.639 for progression only, 0.627 for Status and 0.639 for Timing. The Curie reduced model showed AUC 0.768 after adding status (55 patients, nine deaths), and the OVCAD age–stage model reached 0.747 (152 patients, 19 deaths). The progression-only AUC in OVCAD was 0.794. Newly added absolute risk comparisons varied across cohorts and available predictor sets (Supplementary Table S2). The original Japan age-free Status model reached an AUC of 0.820 and an O/E of 0.68.
H2|Pathology findings and independent tissue cohort
P|Of 347 TCGA landmark patients, 342 had a usable stromal percentage and 49 died by the 12-month horizon. The median was 10% (interquartile range, 5%–17.5%). The same-fold out-of-fold AUC was 0.7721 before adding stroma and 0.7713 afterward. The paired change was −0.0008 (95% CI, −0.0048 to 0.0025), and Brier score increased by 0.0008 (0.0002 to 0.0014). In the pathology-adjusted Cox model, progression status remained associated with death (adjusted HR, 4.30, 95% CI, 2.90–6.40). The hazard ratio for a 10-percentage-point increase in stromal cells was 1.07 (0.92–1.24). Figure 3 shows the source H&E slides and corresponding cohort-level results.
P|In APOLLO-2, 59 participants qualified for landmark analysis, with five deaths in the subsequent year. AUC was 0.528 for Baseline and 0.626 for Status, a paired increase of 0.098 (95% CI, −0.079 to 0.311). Adding Timing left AUC at 0.626. The O/E point estimate was 0.67 (0.15–1.29). The primary-site subset included 44 patients and four deaths. Estimated mortality was 33.3% in six patients with progression and 5.3% in 38 without progression, with wide pointwise intervals (Figure 3). We interpret this comparison as exploratory evidence from a separately assembled dataset.
H1|Discussion
P|Progression status improved the ranking of one-year mortality over a postoperative baseline in the three main external cohorts. When progression status itself was the benchmark, adding age, stage and residual disease gave clear discrimination improvement in one primary external cohort and no consistent improvement in Brier score. For a clinician who already knows that progression has occurred, these analyses illustrate the smaller potential contribution of the exact date under the evaluated specifications. They also show why a model with good discrimination may give poorly portable probabilities.
P|Su and colleagues established evidence for the association of progression status with subsequent OS in pooled first-line trial populations [2]. We extended this question by separating the binary progression indicator from the continuous timing information and testing locked fixed-horizon predictions with calibration in multiple external clinical sources. Studies of prognosis after relapse are informative for patients at that point in their care pathway. Our estimates apply at a fixed month-12 visit to a population containing patients both with and without documented progression. Timing could behave differently under a different visit schedule, treatment era, functional form or target population. Small progressed subgroups and resampling intervals limit an equivalence interpretation.
P|The pathology analysis suggests that the progression association is detectable after adjustment for the available tissue quality-control stromal estimate. The median across sampled TCGA slides is a coarse description of the tissue component and does not capture spatial organization or central histotype review. The two illustrative fields cannot establish prognosis for individual slides and were not used to train a classifier. APOLLO-2 adds pathology-reviewed context, while the five deaths and differing specimen sites restrict any external calibration conclusion. We avoided substituting treatment response labels for the mortality endpoint. For example, PTRC-HGSOC provides digital H&E and platinum-response labels, and SIGNPOST provides post-neoadjuvant chemotherapy response scores and marker measurements. Their available endpoints, patient age coding or ascertainment are unsuitable for direct full-model validation in this analysis [27, 28, 29].
P|For clinical translation, prognosis at a standard visit could help trial stratification or guide prospective research about counseling. The current estimates do not support individual risk advice, more frequent imaging or earlier treatment solely in response to the calculated probability. The randomized early-treatment trial based on a rise in CA-125 illustrates why an earlier indication of relapse does not ensure that acting earlier improves survival [30]. Evaluation in a contemporary cohort should specify a clinical use case, incorporate BRCA/HRD, maintenance treatment and performance status, and separate model recalibration from subsequent validation [16, 31].
P|Limitations include selected tissue-profiled historical cohorts, different progression definitions and clocks, possible participant overlap across some sources without common identifiers, and small event counts in the primary external risk sets and exploratory cohorts. The short public ICON7 follow-up limits longer horizons. Historical serous classifications and grade fields were not centrally harmonized, and several cohorts lacked treatment details, including post-progression therapy. AOCS recorded time in months, as did OVCAD. Different scan intervals and the timing of censoring could misclassify progression near the landmark. Landmark entry excludes patients who died early or whose status was unknown. Model performance at diagnosis is therefore a separate question.
H1|Conclusions
P|At month 12, progression status added reproducible mortality risk discrimination over routine baseline characteristics. The exact progression interval and available TCGA stromal percentage yielded little added discrimination in the analyzed specifications. Absolute risk transport requires validation and calibration in the intended population.
H1|Data and ethics
P|All analyses used existing de-identified source cohorts and open GDC tissue images. The underlying studies report their ethics approvals and patient consent procedures. Data accessions, slide file UUIDs, checksums and field coordinates are provided with the analysis. The original analysis plan and supplementary analysis record distinguish the original contrast from changes introduced during review.
